# Editorial scope and aesthetic delivery

Record the channel subject, intended mathematical understanding, values and applications separately from color, materials, voice, sound and pacing. Respect a user-requested specialist scope. The bundled IVisualizeThings profile is mathematics first: pure mathematics and substantive applications in engineering, computer science, machine learning, physics, daily life, work, optimization and business. Its gentle, elegant, contemplative style serves explanation and appreciation; it does not imply a geometry or wellness channel.

Before writing a batch, inspect available history. For each candidate record its mathematical domain, precise takeaway, why the reasoning matters, application if useful, prerequisite, closest prior mechanism, substantive difference, and proposed visual/narrative treatment. Compare the slate with recent batches: a new formula with the same moving-curve experience can still be repetitive. Titles alone cannot establish novelty inside unavailable historical transcripts.

Select for mathematical value and attainable understanding, then design representations that fit: identified discrete cases, tables, ordered records, timelines, calculations, algorithms or geometry. Do not choose a subject merely because the current renderer makes it easy. A fixed distribution of domains is optional channel policy, not a universal rule.

At editorial review, identify the actual reasoning the viewer can follow. A picturesque analogy or motivational ending does not substitute for it. Keep claims about learning, delight and fear reduction unmeasured until there is audience evidence. Retain useful experiments and known failures in the channel history.

Application areas are examples, not an exhaustive whitelist. Machine learning is explicitly in scope; biology, medicine, music, economics, games, social systems and other fields are welcome when a worthwhile mathematical relationship drives the explanation. Choose for insight and watchability, with accurate assumptions and evidence, rather than expanding into generic commentary about a field.
