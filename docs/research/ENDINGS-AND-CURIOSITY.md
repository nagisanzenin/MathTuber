# Endings that complete an experience

## Problem observed

Cycle six made objects more recognizable, but several final sections held diagrams while narration generalized. A gentle pace need not mean a static conclusion. Our next experiment is a resolved change in the object, with time to notice its meaning.

## Evidence

[Abir and colleagues, PNAS (2026)](https://www.dpmlab.org/papers/abir-et-al-2026-learning-reinforces-curiosity-for-related-information.pdf) studied online choices to wait for answers. More satisfying answers predicted subsequent information seeking particularly for semantically related questions. Individual ratings mattered; group averages did not reproduce the same pattern. Effects for unrelated questions depended on context. Memory effects were more mixed, and prior-trial learning signals did not reliably predict next-trial recall. This was a question-and-answer task, not a test of Shorts, channel subscriptions, or ending styles. Our inference is to satisfy the current question before inviting closely related noticing; it is not evidence that cliffhangers or any particular ending improve retention.

[Generative learning activities for online multimedia learning (2024)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1452385/full) tested undergraduates learning from four animated greenhouse-effect slides. Learners wrote summaries or drew between slides without viewing the material. Summary-only groups outperformed controls on immediate and one-week tests in planned comparisons. Drawing did not show the same advantage; combined activities did not clearly add benefit. The experiments had power limitations and unequal activity time. Enjoyment and interest ratings did not significantly differ. These were learner-created responses taking minutes, not a narrator delivering a closing sentence. A brief invitation in a Short does not establish generative learning or transfer.

## Editorial experiment, not a proven recipe

- Establish a recognizable object and a question with one clear relationship.
- Explain through a visible mechanism, preserving what each mark refers to.
- Near the ending, vary one condition that matters to that same relationship.
- Give an optional, untimed space to notice. Then show and explain the consequence.
- Return to the original object with the new relationship visible. A quiet final image may hold after the consequence is resolved.

Use this only when it improves the subject. Avoid five mechanically identical plots, forced questions, motivational slogans, and withheld answers. No requirement for constant movement: motion should reveal a cause, comparison, or consequence.

## Implementation and evaluation

The plugin now extracts an export-bound ending sheet through the last frame, supplementing authored critical intervals. The reviewer records what changed, whether the consequence is understandable without extra text, and whether the final object is recognizable. This increases review coverage; it does not measure audience satisfaction.

Next batch: fountain trajectories, lantern illumination, water level in a widening vessel, an ideal pulley, and a falling leaf model. Each uses a distinct physical change. Mathematical and modeling assumptions must be independently checked. Compare the final section with the opening and with cycle six's static endings. Audience retention, comprehension, calm, and willingness to explore remain unmeasured unless real data is collected.
