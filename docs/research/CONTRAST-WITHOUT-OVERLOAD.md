# Let the viewer notice a consequential difference

Prepared 6 September 2026 during the cycle-sixteen publication quota wait. This is preproduction guidance for the next cycle, not a claim that another batch has been produced or audience-tested.

## Problem observed

The relations films make their mathematical quantities more explicit, but some passages still consist of a diagram followed by narrated conclusions. The remainder film's opening leaves eight counters almost unchanged for its first three seconds. Its later table communicates the mapping more clearly. These are observations from actual sampled rendered evidence; they do not establish which moments viewers skip. A gentle channel can invite intellectual participation without a countdown, an exam-like interruption or manufactured suspense.

## Evidence and limits

[Brod and Breitwieser (2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41539-019-0056-y) compared generating numerical predictions with generating relevant examples. The preregistered within-subject study analyzed 29 university students after four exclusions. Participants studied 90 isolated numerical facts, rated curiosity and later recalled the numbers. Prediction increased self-rated curiosity; the directional curiosity tests were one-tailed. Overall memory did not differ significantly between conditions (p=.682). Higher curiosity was associated with better memory, which is not proof that a prediction prompt improves mathematical understanding. The short test delay, small sample and isolated-fact material limit transfer to a mathematical film. Results, discussion and methods were read from the article and publisher PDF. This supports testing a modest invitation to predict, not requiring one in every episode or claiming better retention.

[Rittle-Johnson, Star and Durkin's author chapter (2017)](https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-my/my-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/3147/2020/01/RittleJohnson_Star_Durkin_2017_ComparisonChapter.pdf) synthesizes their classroom comparison studies. The reported 70-student equation study found advantages in procedural knowledge and flexibility, without a conceptual-knowledge difference. In another study, novices did better with sequential examples and appeared overwhelmed by comparing unfamiliar methods. A slower intervention covering less material supported flexible procedure use, with other outcomes comparable. The chapter also reports that offering supplemental materials and summer development did not reliably improve outcomes across a school year. Relevant study, prior-knowledge and conclusion sections were read; this is an author synthesis, not a fresh independent replication. These classroom activities included explanation and practice. They do not prove that two side-by-side animations improve Shorts learning. Comparison has a preparation and attention cost.

## Production experiment

Choose one consequential difference the mathematical argument actually explains. Establish the common situation before varying it. Keep the reference quantities identifiable, change one relevant condition, and let the consequence remain visible while the explanation catches up. Two simultaneous panels are optional; preserving one object through a change may be easier to follow.

An invitation can be as small as asking whether changing a unit should change a nearest-neighbor answer. Give the viewer enough information to form a thought. Show the result and explain the calculation; do not leave the answer behind an engagement request. If a question needs too much setup, use a clear demonstration instead. A pause is useful only when the scene gives the viewer something intelligible to inspect.

Prototype the complete comparison before adopting it across five films. Reject a version that introduces two unfamiliar representations at once, makes the unchanged reference disappear, or needs several new definitions to explain its surprise. Preserve the mathematical scope: different procedures, different inputs and different assumptions are different comparisons.

For muted viewing, the important difference must survive in the visible state. Narration should supply meaning and reasoning, while attached labels identify quantities. Sound may clarify an event but cannot be the sole evidence of a mathematical change. Preserve the channel's restrained palette, quiet pacing and object identity without forcing every subject into the same board or graph.

## What would count as improvement

Production checks can establish correct intermediate states, readable correspondence, synchronized speech and an adequately held result. They cannot establish viewer participation. A future viewer study should separately ask what changed, why the answer followed and what would happen in a nearby case, while measuring willingness to continue. Compare matched edits if feasible; topic differences and recommendation traffic confound simple view-count comparisons. No such audience experiment has been performed here.
