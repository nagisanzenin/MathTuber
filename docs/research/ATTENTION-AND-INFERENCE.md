# Attention is a means, not the learning outcome

The nature batch exposed three concrete problems: highlighting changed mathematical geometry, markers detached during a rotation, and narration overstated what an intermediate construction did. Its persistent heading layout also kept too much explanatory text far from the relevant objects. These observations motivate a narrower improvement than simply making the films more animated.

## Evidence

[Hu and Zhang (2021)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.736922/full) studied 61 university students learning tornado formation from four automatically advanced pages over six minutes. Larger explanatory labels improved retention and entries into the cue area; recognition and transfer did not significantly improve. Color had no significant main effect on post-test scores. The implication for us is limited: make necessary labels readable, but do not equate salience or gaze with understanding. This was not a Shorts experiment and does not prescribe our font size or palette.

[Désiron, Bétrancourt and de Vries (2021)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.592509/full) found that cross-representational signals supported local inferences in an adolescent sample. Their second experiment with university students found different picture-viewing behavior without improved comprehension scores. The population and inference task matter. Our hypothesis is that a brief phrase linked to an actual changing object can reduce the work of finding its referent. The film still needs to show why the relationship holds.

[Tversky, Morrison and Bétrancourt (2002)](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/_Morrison_Betrancourt_AnimationCanitfacilitate.pdf) distinguish correspondence between the graphic and the concept from the viewer's ability to perceive and comprehend that graphic. Their review warns that speed, complexity and unequal comparisons can undermine apparent animation benefits. We use this as a design framework, not a current estimate of animation effectiveness: motion should expose the relevant change at a legible pace, with stable states available for comparison.

## Production choices to test

1. Put concise explanatory labels beside their referents when space permits. Narration captions remain in a stable reading area; attached labels should identify relationships rather than duplicate whole sentences.
2. Give each film a composition arising from its subject. A polarizer, track, balancing mobile or overlapping grating can occupy the frame. A top heading is optional; do not replace one rigid template with another.
3. Preserve geometry and identity during attention cues. An emphasis border can surround a value without filling its glyphs; a route can change stroke weight without moving its endpoints. The engine can supply optional primitives while the agent chooses when they help.
4. Inspect the start, midpoint and end of coupled transformations. Include all changing objects in the animation's moving family; a callback mutating an otherwise static object can leave a cached ghost in Manim's renderer.
5. Compare spoken quantifiers with intermediate states. “All,” “always,” “equal” and “shortest” must apply to the object and assumptions actually displayed. A correct final diagram cannot repair an inaccurate sentence.
6. Make the difficult inference visible before its concluding phrase. If the explanation depends on an inequality, projection or averaging, show that operation or explicitly scope the film as intuition rather than a complete derivation.

## Evaluation

Prototype the difficult interval with captions before freezing final inputs. Inspect labels at phone scale and follow the same object through the transition. Keep technical defects, editorial weaknesses and actual audience outcomes separate. A future viewer study should ask what relationship the viewer can explain or use, alongside willingness to continue and the emotional tone they experienced. No such viewer study has been performed here.
