# Let the ending use the idea

Cycle17 inspection found several long concluding formula holds. The next production experiment replaces a repeated summary, where appropriate, with one small changed case using the same representation. This is an editorial response to observed media, not a demonstrated retention intervention.

[Jing, Szpunar and Schacter (2016)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27295464/) report two experiments involving a forty-minute recorded lecture. Their abstract reports improved learning and integration with interpolated memory tests compared with restudy. Experiment1 did not find different overall mind-wandering rates between groups. The abstract was inspected; full methods were not accessed here. This supports investigating active use of learned material, but does not establish the effect of an optional prediction in a short entertainment video.

[Szpunar, Khan and Schacter (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23576743/) report benefits of interpolated tests in online lectures. The accessible abstract was read; attempted full-text access was blocked. Do not infer a particular pause duration, Shorts retention effect or reduction of mathematics anxiety from this evidence.

## Production hypothesis

After demonstrating the central relationship, change one input or assumption. Invite a small prediction without a countdown, score or pressure. Keep the objects and prerequisite information visible. Show the answer and the reason before ending. The change should be solvable from the explanation already given; an unexplained harder problem is not a satisfying conclusion.

The invitation may be omitted if the film already ends with a visible consequence. Preserve the channel's gentle voice and mathematical precision. Do not add a compulsory test to every topic.

## What to record

For each candidate record the original case, changed condition, expected inference, visual evidence of the answer and remaining prerequisite. Review the actual final fifteen seconds. Determine whether a new mathematical consequence is visible, rather than merely whether motion occurs. Audience learning, enjoyment and retention remain unmeasured until suitable data are collected. Future comparisons should hold topic and duration reasonably comparable and assess comprehension separately from watch time.
