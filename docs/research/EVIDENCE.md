# MathTuber research evidence

Targeted evidence synthesis, not a preregistered exhaustive systematic review. Primary papers, author/institution repositories, syntheses, official platform guidance and one explicitly labeled practitioner account. Search snippets used only when identifiable as author/publisher material; access depth recorded. No full copyrighted papers redistributed.

Study quality and relevance to math Shorts are separate. Effect sizes are study summaries, not additive gains or predicted channel lift. Proposed design rules require calibration.

**E01 — [Noetel et al. (2022): Multimedia Design for Learning: An Overview of Reviews With Meta-Meta-Analysis](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211052329)**

Design: review of reviews. Access: publisher abstract and metadata.

29 reviews encompassing 1,189 studies and 78,177 participants; positive effects for multiple design principles.

Limits: Broad instructional multimedia, not specifically feed-based math videos; overlapping syntheses are not independent replications.

**E02 — [Fyfield, Henderson & Phillips (2022): Improving instructional video design: A systematic review](https://ajet.org.au/index.php/AJET/article/view/7296)**

Design: systematic review. Access: publisher abstract.

113 papers, 28 principles; coherence, segmentation and learner control supported across video contexts; modality and redundancy not supported as general video rules.

Limits: Heterogeneous methods; no dependable universal timing threshold or ranking of principles.

**E03 — [Guo, Kim & Rubin (2014): How Video Production Affects Student Engagement](https://juhokim.com/files/LAS2014-Engagement.pdf)**

Design: observational study. Access: author-hosted paper excerpts.

6.9 million watching sessions in four edX courses; shorter videos and certain presentation styles associated with greater engagement.

Limits: MOOC engagement proxies, observational confounding, older desktop context; not proof of a six-minute cognitive limit or optimal Shorts length.

**E04 — [Gruber, Gelman & Ranganath (2014): States of Curiosity Modulate Hippocampus-Dependent Learning](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2zd605r7)**

Design: laboratory experiment. Access: author repository abstract.

High-curiosity states associated with better immediate and next-day memory for trivia answers and incidental information.

Limits: Laboratory trivia and memory, not a causal test of video hooks, virality or a dopamine-based editing recipe.

**E05 — [Silvia (2005): What Is Interesting? Exploring the Appraisal Structure of Interest](https://libres.uncg.edu/ir/uncg/f/P_Silvia_What_2005.pdf)**

Design: experimental research and theory. Access: author manuscript indexed text.

Interest modeled through novelty/complexity and perceived capacity to understand.

Limits: Stimuli and appraisals differ from mathematical Shorts; neither universal optimal complexity nor cut timing established.

**E06 — [Muller et al. (2008): Saying the wrong thing: improving learning with multimedia by including misconceptions](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2729.2007.00248.x)**

Design: randomized experiment. Access: publisher abstract.

364 first-year physics students; misconception refutation and dialogue produced greater conceptual gains than concise exposition in this experiment.

Limits: Physics instruction and pre/post conceptual tests; not evidence that misleading hooks or prolonged confusion improve feed retention.

**E07 — [Sinha & Kapur (2021): When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Works](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105)**

Design: meta-analysis. Access: publisher excerpts and author abstract in ERIC.

53 studies, 166 comparisons; overall g=0.36 favoring problem solving before instruction, with implementation-dependent outcomes.

Limits: Structured problem-solving and consolidation; a brief rhetorical question is not the same intervention.

**E08 — [Lodge et al. (2018): Understanding Difficulties and Resulting Confusion in Learning](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2018.00049/full)**

Design: integrative review. Access: full text sections.

Confusion may support learning when resolved with suitable guidance; persistent unresolved confusion can become frustration or boredom.

Limits: Effects depend on prior knowledge, task and support. Deliberately obscuring an explanation is not justified.

**E09 — [Höffler & Leutner (2007): Instructional animation versus static pictures: A meta-analysis](https://www.leibniz-ipn.de/en/research/publications/instructional-animation-versus-static-pictures-a-meta-analysis)**

Design: meta-analysis. Access: institutional abstract.

26 studies, 76 comparisons; overall d=0.37 favoring animation, with variation by representational purpose and task.

Limits: Moderators can co-vary; this does not prescribe photorealism for abstract mathematics.

**E10 — [Berney & Bétrancourt (2016): Does animation enhance learning? A meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131516301336)**

Design: meta-analysis. Access: publisher results excerpts.

61 experiments, 140 comparisons, 7,036 participants; overall animation benefit but most individual comparisons not statistically significant.

Limits: Animation is conditional, not automatically superior to a well-designed static display.

**E11 — [Tversky, Morrison & Bétrancourt (2002): Animation: can it facilitate?](https://faculty.washington.edu/aragon/classes/hcde411/w13/readings/Tversky_AnimationFacilitate_IJHCS02.pdf)**

Design: review and design theory. Access: paper excerpts.

Graphics should match the concept and remain perceivable and interpretable; transient motion can undermine comprehension.

Limits: Foundational theory, not an experimentally validated Shorts production specification.

**E12 — [Soderstrom & Bjork (2015): Learning Versus Performance: An Integrative Review](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/11/soderstorm_ra_learningvsperformance.pdf)**

Design: integrative review. Access: author-hosted paper excerpts.

Immediate performance and subjective fluency can diverge from durable retention and transfer.

Limits: Watching time, confidence and immediate quizzes alone cannot establish long-term learning.

**E13 — [Szpunar, Khan & Schacter (2013): Interpolated memory tests reduce mind wandering and improve learning of online lectures](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1221764110)**

Design: two experiments. Access: publisher methods and abstract.

Tests between lecture segments reduced mind wandering and improved learning in the studied online lecture setting.

Limits: Roughly 22-minute lecture with actual responses; silent prediction in a Short is a proposed adaptation.

**E14 — [Bisra et al. (2018): Inducing Self-Explanation: A Meta-Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x)**

Design: meta-analysis. Access: author abstract via ERIC.

69 effects from 64 reports; mean g=0.55 for self-explanation prompts.

Limits: Learners actually generated explanations; merely showing a prompt does not demonstrate participation.

**E15 — [Dahlstrom (2014): Using narratives and storytelling to communicate science with nonexpert audiences](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1320645111)**

Design: research synthesis. Access: publisher full text sections.

Narrative organizes causality, events and characters; can engage nonexperts but also gives individual examples persuasive force.

Limits: Narrative is not evidence of a general theorem; salient anecdotes can distort statistical understanding.

**E16 — [Berger & Milkman (2012): What Makes Online Content Viral?](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353)**

Design: observational analysis and experiments. Access: publisher abstract.

Emotional activation associated with sharing in news data, with experimental support for effects on transmission.

Limits: News/email and experimental transmission are not math Shorts retention or learning; emotion types are not interchangeable.

**E17 — [Brom, Stárková & D’Mello (2018): How effective is emotional design?](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X18302148)**

Design: meta-analysis. Access: publisher discussion and conclusion.

Pleasant colors and/or anthropomorphic instructional graphics showed learning benefits; affective mediation was less settled.

Limits: Specific design manipulations; no universal best palette, mascot or rationale for unrelated decoration.

**E18 — [Sundararajan & Adesope (2020): Keep It Coherent: A Meta-Analysis of the Seductive Details Effect](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09522-4)**

Design: meta-analysis. Access: author abstract via ERIC.

Interesting irrelevant details can impair learning; effects vary by material and methodology.

Limits: Does not imply every beautiful, humorous or emotionally meaningful element is irrelevant.

**E19 — [Johnson & Steinerberger (2019): Intuitions about mathematical beauty](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31015078/)**

Design: three studies. Access: author abstract.

Laypeople made systematic aesthetic judgments of simple mathematical arguments; elegance, profundity and clarity related to judgments.

Limits: Selected arguments and judgments, not evidence of universal taste or increased watch time.

**E20 — [Hidi & Renninger (2006): The Four-Phase Model of Interest Development](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_4)**

Design: theoretical synthesis. Access: publisher abstract.

Distinguishes triggered and maintained situational interest from developing individual interest.

Limits: A framework for development, not a validated subscriber conversion model.

**E21 — [Bunquin & Cohen (2026): When scientists ’Tok](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/09636625261469057)**

Design: observational content analysis. Access: publisher and PubMed abstracts.

1,025 science TikToks from 113 creators; humor and imitation positively associated with engagement metrics in multilevel models.

Limits: Associations, not causal effects; no comprehension test and no proof that adding memes improves mathematics learning.

**E22 — [Facilitating knowledge construction in informal learning (2023): A study of TikTok scientific, educational videos](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131523001732)**

Design: observational analysis with classifiers. Access: publisher abstract.

114 videos and 259,508 comments; most comments social; informational content, original audio and analytic language associated with more knowledge-related discussion.

Limits: Classifier labels and commenter self-selection; discussion is not representative viewer learning.

**E23 — [Rein (2023): Making Science Education More Accessible](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37640135/)**

Design: single-channel case study. Access: author abstract.

Analysis of 150 sampled science TikToks explores associations among video characteristics and engagement.

Limits: Successful single creator; selection and platform effects limit generalization.

**E24 — [The Reel Deal (2025): Designing and Evaluating LLM-Generated Short-Form Educational Videos](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05962v1)**

Design: between-subject user study; arXiv version. Access: full text including limitations.

62 university students, introductory programming; structured generated reels improved immediate quizzes and efficiency relative to selected longer videos.

Limits: Bundle of format/content changes; brief quiz, limited population, no durable-learning test. Do not infer a pure duration effect.

**E25 — [Cristobal et al. (2026): PaperTok: Exploring the Use of Generative AI for Creating Short-form Videos for Research Communication](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18218v1)**

Design: CHI 2026 mixed-methods study. Access: full text sections; DOI listed in manuscript.

Formative N=8, researchers N=18 and audience N=100; tested an iterative AI authoring system, highlighting perceived engagement and demand for granular control.

Limits: Comparisons with other generation tools; perceived informational value is not measured mathematical transfer.

**E26 — [Zhang et al. (2026): Educational Short Videos: Bibliometric Trends, Thematic Structure, and Operationalisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06932)**

Design: bibliometric mapping; preprint. Access: abstract.

2,169 indexed records through June 11, 2026; inconsistent operationalization of shortness and outcomes.

Limits: Maps the field rather than estimates causal effectiveness. Preprint status.

**E27 — [YouTube Help (accessed 2026-09-05): Search and discovery tips — Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en-GB)**

Design: official platform documentation. Access: full page.

Ranking uses watch choice, viewing duration/percentage and satisfaction signals, with personalization and competition.

Limits: No public universal winning threshold, exact weighting or guarantee of distribution.

**E28 — [YouTube Help (accessed 2026-09-05): Understand your YouTube content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop&hl=en-9)**

Design: official metric documentation. Access: page excerpts.

Shorts average duration and percentage viewed use engaged views and corresponding watch time.

Limits: Denominators differ from all feed exposures; do not equate completion among stayers with reach or understanding.

**E29 — [Cheah et al. (2022): Background Music and Cognitive Task Performance](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20592043221134392)**

Design: systematic review. Access: publisher text excerpts.

Music effects depend on tasks, music and populations; lyrics and demanding tasks can be problematic.

Limits: Cognitive-task performance is not the whole audiovisual entertainment experience.

**E30 — [de la Mora Velasco et al. (2023): The impact of background music on learners](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/03057356231153070)**

Design: meta-analysis. Access: publisher abstract.

47 studies, 71 effects, overall d=0.314 favoring background music across heterogeneous learning settings.

Limits: Timing, tasks and music differ; does not establish that continuous music improves narrated mathematical explanations.

**E31 — [Moreno & Mayer (2000): A coherence effect in multimedia learning: The case for minimizing irrelevant sounds](https://tecfa.unige.ch/tecfa/teaching/methodo/Moreno_Mayer00.pdf)**

Design: experiments. Access: paper results excerpts.

Extraneous audio harmed some retention and transfer outcomes; sound-effect results varied across experiments.

Limits: Older software and particular sounds; not a ban on sparse, meaningful event sounds.

**E32 — [Ansani et al. (2020): How Soundtracks Shape What We See](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7575867/)**

Design: between-subject experiment. Access: paper methods and abstract excerpts.

N=118, alternative soundtracks for a film scene changed aspects of interpretation and perception.

Limits: Film emotion and character interpretation; no math-learning claim.

**E33 — [Marty-Dugas et al. (2024): Instructor enthusiasm in online lectures](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2024.1339815/full)**

Design: two randomized studies. Access: full methods, results and limitations.

Vocal enthusiasm increased reported engagement; second study increased intention to watch another lecture; no conclusive quiz benefit.

Limits: 22-minute university lecture; intentions are not actual returns. Null quiz result is not proof of zero effect.

**E34 — [I am Alexa, your virtual tutor! (2022): Text-to-speech voice enthusiasm in multimedia learning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9361884/)**

Design: experiment. Access: indexed author text excerpts.

Synthetic speech can convey emotional tone; investigated enthusiasm as a multimedia design feature.

Limits: Specific engine and setting; cannot rank current local TTS systems or validate present-day voice quality.

**E35 — [Smith (2012): The Attentional Theory of Cinematic Continuity](https://ualresearchonline.arts.ac.uk/id/eprint/21187/)**

Design: film-perception theory. Access: author repository abstract and manuscript excerpts.

Attention and perceptual expectations help explain continuity across cuts.

Limits: Film theory informs a design analogy; no fixed edit rate established for mathematics.

**E36 — [Silas et al. (accepted version accessed 2026-09-05): The Recall and Recognition of Sonic Logos](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/903c50f3-6d66-4721-af94-7c037073b139)**

Design: experimental research. Access: institutional abstract; full text embargoed.

90 sonic logos, N=1,860; recognition and sung recall reflect different aspects of melodic structure.

Limits: Sonic-logo memory is not channel loyalty; best signature must be tested in context.

**E37 — [W3C WAI (accessed 2026-09-05): Understanding Captions (Prerecorded)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Understanding/captions-prerecorded.html)**

Design: accessibility guidance. Access: full page.

Synchronized captions make spoken and meaningful non-speech audio accessible.

Limits: Accessibility rationale, not an empirical engagement optimization study.

**E38 — [Zheng et al. (2023): Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)**

Design: benchmark research. Access: author abstract.

Documents position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases in model judging alongside useful agreement in studied tasks.

Limits: Chat-response judgments do not validate simulated human responses to audiovisual entertainment.

**E39 — [Take caution in using LLMs as human surrogates (2025): Human-surrogate validation research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12184514/)**

Design: empirical research. Access: indexed author text excerpts.

Compares model response distributions with human data and identifies limits to substitution.

Limits: Specific tasks and models; supports calibration requirements rather than claims about all future models.

**E40 — [3Blue1Brown (2021): SoME1 results](https://www.3blue1brown.com/blog/some1-results/)**

Design: practitioner account. Access: creator article.

Highlights motivation, clarity, topic selection and non-obvious insights in mathematical exposition.

Limits: Expert creative judgment and selected examples; not a controlled test of causal effectiveness.

**E41 — [Understand three-minute YouTube Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)**

Current square/vertical Shorts limit is 180 seconds. Over-one-minute Shorts with active copyright claims are blocked. Platform constraint, not an optimal educational duration; recheck when publishing.
