# Make a mathematical state change readable

The applications batch exposed an actionable weakness: an old answer could survive briefly while the next example was already visible. Correct endpoints and valid code were insufficient. The repair was to retire the old claim before the changed object, then reveal the updated result. This is a production observation, not evidence of audience improvement.

Tversky, Morrison and Betrancourt's2002 review distinguishes correspondence between a graphic and its intended concept from the viewer's ability to perceive and interpret it. It questions blanket advantages of animation and notes comparison confounds involving extra information or interactivity. This supports asking what each movement communicates and whether its intermediate states are readable. It does not establish a Shorts pacing formula or prove that animation always outperforms a static diagram. [Author-hosted paper, abstract and graphics discussion](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/_Morrison_Betrancourt_AnimationCanitfacilitate.pdf).

The next production experiment uses smaller state changes, explicitly introduced representations, and an optional final changed case. A probability-scoring film must identify outcome and forecast before combining their errors; a binary-game film must teach its column operation before naming a strategy. These are design hypotheses to inspect, not automatic acceptance criteria.
