# Everyday objects, mathematical relationships

Five original films explore beats, cooling tea, similar shadows, apparent depth, and average walking speed. Recognizable objects lead into explicitly corresponding mathematical diagrams. See [recognition and listening research](../../docs/research/RECOGNITION-AND-LISTENING.md).

Initialize a project from its manifest, copy scenes and JSON assets, synthesize speech, and compile cues. For the beats project, run `build_tones.py PROJECT_PATH` with the media Python after speech synthesis and before rendering; it recreates the original tone score and its exact listening windows. For the other projects, use the standard sound-event compiler. Inspect captioned previews, then render, assemble and review all final evidence before publishing.

The beats scene has two selected paragraph pauses; other boundaries remain short. Its drawn carrier waves are schematic; the listening-window circles follow the actual tone envelope. Cooling assumes constant surroundings and a constant factor. Shadows require upright objects and level ground at the same sun angle. Apparent depth uses a small-angle approximation. Walking compares equal distances before contrasting equal times.

Math checks are independent of rendered labels. Media, credentials and publication intents are excluded. Signal analysis and image sequences do not establish subjective sound quality, viewer learning or engagement.
